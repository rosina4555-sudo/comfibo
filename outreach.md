# WhatsApp Outreach — Business Contact Flow

Guidelines and paste-ready messages for contacting businesses found on Google Maps
about the Small Business Website Initiative.

---

## Ground rules

1. **Lead with how you found them** — it explains why a stranger is texting.
2. **First message ≤ 60 words, one ask.** Nothing else.
3. **No link in message one.** Links only after they say yes.
4. **Say why it's free in one line.** Silence here reads as a scam.
5. **Give an easy out** ("no pressure either way") — this is what gets replies.
6. **Max two follow-ups**, 3–4 days apart, then stop permanently.
7. **Timing:** Tue–Thu, 9–11am or 2–4pm their local time. Never Monday morning.
8. **One business at a time.** Personalise every first line.
9. **If you're solo, say "I run Comfibo."** Don't imply a team you don't have.

---

## Stage 1 — Opener

```
Hello [Business Name]

I came across your business on Google Maps while looking at businesses in [Area].

I run Comfibo, a web design studio. We're taking on a small number of businesses
to build a professional website at no cost — we're building our portfolio, so the
development work is free on your end.

Is a short chat worthwhile? No pressure either way.
```

Swap `[Area]` for something real — *"while looking at catering businesses in Lekki."*
That one detail proves you actually looked them up.

---

## Stage 2 — They say "yes, tell me more"

```
Thank you — here's the short version.

We design and build a professional website for your business: home, about,
services and contact pages, mobile-friendly, with WhatsApp or a contact form.

Why it's free: we're building our public portfolio. You get the site at no
development cost, we get permission to show the finished work. That's the whole
exchange — no hidden charge on the build.

Domain and hosting are separate and paid directly to the provider. We'll tell you
the exact cost before anything is agreed.

Two quick questions:
1. Do you currently have a website?
2. What should it do best — get calls, show your services, or look more credible?
```

Answer those two well and you'll know if they qualify.

---

## Stage 3 — They want to proceed

```
Good fit. Next step is a short application so I have everything in one place:

[your /initiative link]

It takes about 5 minutes. I review it and reply with either a yes or an honest
no — you won't be left waiting.

If you'd rather speak first, I'm free [day] between [time]. Whatever suits you.
```

---

## Stage 4 — No reply

**Follow-up 1 (day 3–4):**

```
Hello — following up on my message about a free website for [Business Name].

If the timing's wrong or you're not interested, that's completely fine. Just say
so and I won't follow up again.
```

**Follow-up 2 (day 10), then stop:**

```
Last note from me — I'll close this off after today.

If you ever want a site done properly, my number is saved here. All the best with
[Business Name].
```

## Objections

| They say | You send |
| --- | --- |
| "How is it free?" | `Because I'm building my portfolio. You get the site free, I get to show it publicly. No charge on the development.` |
| "What's the catch?" | `No catch — the build is free in exchange for permission to showcase it. You keep full ownership of the site and the domain.` |
| "I already have a website" | `Even better. I'll review it free of charge and tell you honestly what's costing you customers — and if nothing's wrong, I'll say that too.` |
| "Send me details" | `Happy to: [link]. Take a read — I'll leave it with you and you can reply here whenever.` |
| "I don't have budget" | `That's exactly why I messaged. The build is free. Only domain/hosting is paid, and that's your decision later, not now.` |
| "How do I know you're real?" | `Fair question. You can see our work at [link], and I'm happy to take a call. Nothing is paid up front — you pay nothing for the build.` |
| "Not interested" | `Understood, thank you for your time. I won't message again. All the best with [Business Name].` — **send this, and actually stop** |

## Phone calls

Use this when a message gets no reply. The message's job changes — it's no
longer asking for a reply, it's explaining why you're ringing so they pick up.

### Send this before you ring

```
Hello [Business Name], Emmanuel here from Comfibo — a web design studio.

I found your business on Google Maps while looking at businesses in [Area].
I'd like to call you for two minutes about building your website at no cost.

I'll ring in a moment. If it's a bad time, just say so.
```

Then call within a minute or two.

### On the phone — first 15 seconds

> "Hello, am I speaking with **[Name / the owner of Business Name]**?
> This is **Emmanuel from Comfibo** — I sent you a message on WhatsApp just now.
> Is now a good time? I'll only take a minute."

Always ask *"Is now a good time?"* — letting them say no makes them far more
likely to listen when they do say yes.

### The pitch (about 30 seconds)

> "I'll be brief. I came across your business on Google Maps while looking at
> businesses in **[Area]**, and I run a web design studio called Comfibo.
>
> We're taking on a small number of businesses to build a professional website
> — home, about, services and contact pages, mobile-friendly.
>
> **The build itself is free.** We're assembling our portfolio, so there's no
> development cost to you. The only thing we ask is permission to show the
> finished site in our work. Domain and hosting you'd pay the provider
> directly, and we'd tell you that cost before anything is agreed."

Then stop talking and ask one open question:

> **"Has that come at a good time, or have you already got a site you're happy with?"**

Both answers are useful. If they have a site: *"May I ask what it's doing for
you — are you getting enquiries through it?"*

### How to end — pick one

**They're interested:**

> "I won't keep you any longer. I'll put the details in writing on WhatsApp,
> plus a short form that takes five minutes. Have a look whenever suits you and
> reply on WhatsApp. All the best with **[Business Name]**."

**They're unsure:**

> "No pressure at all. I'll send you a written summary on WhatsApp so it's not
> just a phone call you're trying to remember. Read it when you get a chance —
> you can ignore it completely if it's not for you."

**They're not interested:**

> "I appreciate your time, thank you. I won't follow up — you won't hear from
> me again. All the best with **[Business Name]**."

Then **actually stop**. That's what makes the next one easier.

**They say "send me something":**

> "Will do. I'll send it within the hour — and I'll follow up on **[day]** just
> in case you have questions."

Always name a day. It turns a vague maybe into a real step.

### Voicemail

> "Hello, this is **Emmanuel from Comfibo** calling for **[Business Name]** —
> I sent you a message on WhatsApp about building your website at no cost.
> I'll try again another time, or you can reply to that message. Thank you."

Then don't call the same day.

### Five rules while you're new

1. **Say your name and Comfibo in the first sentence.** Never open with a pitch.
2. **Talk 40%, listen 60%.** Their answers tell you if they qualify.
3. **One idea, not a feature list.** Free build + portfolio + ownership. Stop.
4. **Stand up and smile.** It changes how you sound more than any script.
5. **Call Tue–Thu, 10am–1pm or 2–4pm.** Not Monday, not Friday afternoon.

Have a pen ready — write down what they say. It makes the WhatsApp follow-up
specific.

---

## Never do these

- Blast numbers you didn't personally open on Google Maps
- Send a link in the first message
- Claim clients, results or a team you don't have
- Invent urgency ("only 2 spots left!" when untrue)
- Message someone again after they opt out
- Share their number or details with anyone

## Before you hit send

- [ ] Business name spelled exactly as listed
- [ ] Area/industry mentioned in line 2 is accurate
- [ ] Number is the business's listed contact, not a personal one
- [ ] Nothing in the message is a claim you can't back up

---

## Pitching paid work instead

Keep everything, change one line — replace the free/portfolio sentence with:

```
We take on a limited number of projects each quarter. I noticed [specific thing
on their listing] and think we could improve how that converts to enquiries.
Worth five minutes?
```
